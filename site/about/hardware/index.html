About · The house

The hardware

562 12V pixels along the roofline, one controller in a weatherproof box on the ground, and two power supplies. Here's how it's wired, what it draws, and where it's going next.

The roofline

562 12V pixels (WS2811) in six straight runs along the roofline, driven by FPP on a Kulp K8-PB. Data leaves the controller at the back-left corner of the porch on two ports, each a chain of three runs. Every pixel sits at the same height, so from above the roofline is a flat outline: two wings come forward toward the street with the porch set back between them.

Each port is fed 12V at the controller end, and an injection line runs to its far end so the last pixels can stay as bright and as white as the first. For now the injection lines are unplugged while I track down a fault in them, and the house runs from the controller end alone, well within what the ports can carry.

The controller box

The controller, its power and every connection live in one IP66 box that sits on the ground at the back-left corner of the porch, with all the cables coming in through glands in one wall. Inside are the Kulp K8-PB, a Faststorm fuse block and two Mean Well LRS-350-12 supplies (12V, 29A each). The first supply powers the controller and, through its own 5A fuse, the far-end injection of each port. The second is for the expansion: its AC is already wired and its negative is tied to the first supply's, so it can take on more injection without rewiring the box.

All eight of the controller's ports are wired out, though the house uses two. Each port sits behind its own electronic fuse, and the spare six are there for when the denser runs need spreading out.

Power

I measured the draw with the controller's own port sensors. A pixel draws about 45 mA at full white (at the 80% brightness the house runs at), and it scales in a straight line with how bright each colour is. So the soft loops that keep the house lit all night cost very little, and the bright moments of a show are where the current goes.

  • 45 mAone pixel at full white
  • 2–7 Wan all-night loop, a few cents a night
  • 3.8 Athe busiest port at the peak of a show
  • ~310 Wthe whole house at full white, fully fed

That last number is the one to plan around. Each end of a port is protected at about 5 A, and a fully fed port at full white wants 12 to 14 A, 6 to 7 A at each end. Today the runs can't draw that much because they only get power from one end, but once the injection goes back in, the brightest frames need a cap on each port's draw so a show never trips a fuse.

Going to 2 inches

I'm moving the roofline onto a new metal rail with a pixel every 2 inches instead of 3, a run at a time: about 750 pixels when it's done, half as many again as the house started with. Porch Front went first, then Porch Right. The shows don't need rewriting: they place everything by run names and distances, so each one fills in the new pixels on the next build.

Parts

  • Kulp K8-PBPixel controller on a PocketBeagle 2, 8 ports, running FPP
  • 12V WS2811 pixels562 today, on 3" and 2" spacing
  • 2× Mean Well LRS-350-1212V, 29A supplies: one for the controller and injection, one for the expansion
  • Faststorm 6-way fuse block5A blade fuses on each injection line
  • QILIPSU IP66 boxPC/ABS, every cable through glands in one wall
  • Falcon F-AmpsOne at the start of each port, boosting the data for the long lead to the first run
  • 18 AWG cable3-conductor for the ports, 2-conductor for injection; 16 AWG for the longest leads

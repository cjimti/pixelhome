About

Scripts, not black boxes

I'm Craig Johnston. Pixelhome is the light show on my house's roofline. Every frame of light and every note of music on this site is computed by a Python script that AI and I wrote together. Even the voice that introduces each Halloween show starts from mine: an open-source model reads it from a few seconds of me speaking, with a bit of an accent added.

Why

I've been a software developer for over 30 years, and I've fully embraced AI. But generative AI is often all or nothing: change one parameter, wait for a black box, and get back a whole new finished result. As of 2026 I only have one string of pixels around my house, and I wanted to get the most out of it.

So rather than asking AI to generate light sequences, I have it write configurable Python scripts that compute them. The music works the same way: Python writes each score note by note and plays it through great open-source instruments like the Surge XT free and open-source hybrid synthesizer and the VSCO 2 Community Edition orchestral samples. When something's off, I change a number, not a prompt.

How a show gets made

Each sequence is one Python file with two functions that share the same timings. render() computes the lights and score() writes the music, so every hit lands on its flash and is panned to where it happens on the house. A VOICEOVER line gives it a spoken intro.

The house itself comes from two exceptional open-source projects: I laid out the pixels in xLights, and modeled the house in Blender. Pixelhome imports both, so every sequence knows where each pixel sits in 3D.

Spoken intros

One string of pixels is abstract. A shape moving along the roofline could be a bat, a train or a skeleton, and from the street you can't always tell which. So, the way an opera is introduced with a synopsis, each Halloween show can open with a short spoken intro: a line to set the mood, then what happens and where to look.

The voice is based on mine, modified with a bit of an accent. I recorded a few seconds of myself reading, and Chatterbox, Resemble AI's open-source text-to-speech model, reads each intro in that voice on my Mac. It's mixed over a quiet bed of choir, cello, music box and a distant bell, and while it speaks the house shows the voice as a red waveform growing out from the middle of the left wing, the porch front and the right wing.

On this site, the Intros switch next to Sound plays a sequence's intro before the show. For FPP, each intro is its own FSEQ and MP3, scheduled just before the show it introduces.

Open source

This site and everything behind it is open source on GitHub. Everything is tailored to my pixel runs and roofline. In the future I may make it easy to adjust the segments to fit other people's setups, and I plan on adding a lot more pixels.

The hardware

501 12V pixels (WS2811) in six straight runs along the roofline, driven by FPP on a Raspberry Pi 4 with a Falcon PiCap v2. Data leaves the controller at the back-left corner of the porch on two ports. One 12V supply powers the controller end, and power is injected again at the far end of each string so the last pixels stay as bright and as white as the first.
